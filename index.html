What’s included—and what isn’t?

These figures use the 101 provided REcolorado listings (62 active, 14 pending, 25 closed). Closing prices, concessions and sale-to-original-list comparisons are calculated only on the 25 closed listings. Days on market for sold homes are measured from those same 25 rows. Pending prices are not known final sale prices. The export includes no final pre-contract asking-price history for closed homes, so a reliable average discount against final list price cannot be calculated. Some original-list-to-close differences may reflect earlier price reductions.

05 / What this means for your move

Patience and preparation can work together.

Watch the slow movers.

Longer listing times often create better conversations about price, closing costs, or repairs. But a long-listed home also deserves closer inspection to understand why it has lingered.

Keep the main-level layout first.

A 3,000-square-foot home with only 1,350 square feet upstairs can live very differently from a 2,000-square-foot ranch. The finished-basement number is useful, but not the whole story.

Compare the real monthly cost.

Castle Rock and Parker can vary considerably in taxes, metro districts and HOA fees. A lower sticker price doesn’t always mean a lower monthly obligation.

Three homes that illustrate the tradeoffs

2311 Canyonlands Way ($649k): lower than the preferred ceiling, but only 1,349 square feet are above grade. 11894 Pitchfork Court ($679k): 2,044 square feet on the main level and a larger yard; built in 2005. 3726 Outcrop Court ($714.9k): newer 2022 construction and 1,925 main-level square feet, but higher reported annual taxes. Different strengths, different compromises—and exactly why we’re studying them now.
